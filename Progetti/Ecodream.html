01. Overview

Ecodream is a sustainable brand that aims to produce eco-friendly bags and accessories while promoting environmental responsibility.
Its strengths lie in the message it wants to communicate, in its local production, and in the uniqueness and quality of its products.

However, the current e-commerce website has usability and accessibility issues that make it difficult to navigate and use, contributing to lower conversion rates.

The goal of this project was to redesign the full e-commerce experience without losing the essence of the brand.

02. The Problem

The experience lacked clear information architecture and wayfinding.
Without breadcrumbs, effective filters, or a consistent visual hierarchy, users struggled to understand where they were and how to reach key content.
Important pages required unnecessary steps, interactive elements were difficult to identify, and repetitive copy increased cognitive load, making browsing slower and less intuitive.

  • ×Unclear Information Architecture
  • ×Limited Navigation and Discoverability
  • ×Weak Content Hierarchy and Interaction Clarity

03. Proposed Solution

Redesign the EcoDream shopping experience to make it clearer, more accessible, and easier to navigate, without losing the brand’s handcrafted identity.

  1. Simplify the structure Create a clearer information architecture with consistent navigation and breadcrumbs.
  2. Improve product discovery Introduce search and filters to help customers find relevant products more efficiently.
  3. Build clarity and trust Reorganize product, policy, and sustainability information within a stronger visual hierarchy.

These improvements would help customers understand EcoDream’s value and purchase with more confidence.

04. Research Process

I evaluated the end-to-end shopping experience to identify where navigation, accessibility, and content discovery were creating friction.

What I did

  1. Heuristic evaluation and accessibility audit
  2. Competitive benchmark — 7 brands
  3. User survey — 55 participants
  4. Personas and journey mapping

What I discovered

  1. Participants struggled to locate products and essential information.
  2. Missing search, filters, and breadcrumbs made browsing inefficient.
  3. Scattered and repetitive content increased cognitive load.
  4. Unclear product and policy information reduced user confidence.

I conducted a heuristic evaluation across desktop and mobile, using Nielsen’s 10 usability principles
to identify recurring friction and translate the findings into clear redesign priorities.

  1. 01

    Navigation & wayfinding

    Current experience

    Missing breadcrumbs, unclear active states, incomplete navigation, and inconsistent cart visibility made it difficult to maintain context and move efficiently across the website.

    Proposed experience

    Simplify the global navigation, introduce breadcrumbs and clear active states, and keep essential controls—such as the cart—consistently visible.

  2. 02

    Product discovery & efficiency

    Current experience

    The absence of search and filters, combined with long pages and hidden customization options, made relevant products harder to find.

    Proposed experience

    Add search, filters, and clearer product categories while making customization options easier to discover and access.

  3. 03

    Interaction clarity & system feedback

    Current experience

    Clickable elements and calls to action were not always clearly distinguishable, while feedback, confirmation, recovery, and error states were inconsistent.

    Proposed experience

    Strengthen visual affordances, clarify calls to action, and provide timely confirmations, actionable error messages, and recovery options.

  4. 04

    Content hierarchy & support

    Current experience

    Important information was fragmented, repeated, or difficult to scan. Unclear terminology, an incomplete footer, and the absence of FAQs further increased the effort required to find support.

    Proposed experience

    Consolidate repetitive content, establish a clearer hierarchy, and surface product, policy, sustainability, and support information where customers expect to find it.

05. Design Process

I translated the research findings into a clearer, intuitive and more accessible shopping experience,
moving from information architecture and wireframes to a responsive interface and reusable design system.

What I did

  1. Restructured the information architecture
  2. Created desktop and mobile wireframes for five key pages, focusing on navigation and product discovery.
  3. Defined the visual foundations and reusable UI components
  4. Designed and prototyped the 5 pages.

The original site scattered related content across multiple pages and provided limited access to key shopping functions.
I reorganized the information architecture around users’ primary goals: understanding the brand, exploring the catalogue, and finding essential purchase information.

Key Changes

  1. Simplified global navigation Grouped related content into clearer, more predictable sections.

  2. Reorganised product discovery Created a linear browsing structure: product type → model → individual product.

  3. Improved access to key functions Made search, wishlist, cart, account, customer support, and policy information easier to reach.

Wireframing

Using the revised information architecture as a foundation, I developed responsive wireframes for five key pages. I applied familiar e-commerce patterns and progressive disclosure to simplify navigation, reduce the effort required to find and compare products, and surface decision-critical information at the right moment.

I created a project-level design system combining visual foundations, reusable components, interaction states,
and usage guidelines to maintain consistency across desktop and mobile.

06. Prototype & Usability Testing

I tested the mobile prototype with seven participants to evaluate whether the redesigned navigation and product-discovery flow supported four core tasks.
Remote, unmoderated sessions were recorded in Maze and reviewed for completion, misclicks, and perceived ease of use.

All tasks were completed, but two journeys produced high misclick rates and revealed avoidable friction.

Key Findings & Design Response

  1. Company information remained a bit difficult to locate

    Although the task was completed successfully, it took 60.9 seconds on average and recorded a 77.9% misclick rate.

    Design response

    Make company information more visible by adding a dedicated homepage call to action and improving navigation labels.

  2. Users expected a search function

    Six of seven participants selected search as their first action, and every participant interacted with it at least once.

    Design response

    Implement and validate the complete search and results flow.

07. Outcomes & Reflection

Testing validated the overall direction of the redesigned experience while identifying the areas that still require refinement.
Participants completed all four assigned tasks and rated the overall experience positively. However, testing revealed remaining friction around search and company-information discovery, helping define clear priorities for the next iteration.

Next iteration

  1. Validate search, sitemap, and wishlist interactions.
  2. Make company information more visible, including a homepage call to action.
  3. Surface shop subcategories directly in the main navigation.
  4. Reduce misclicks through clearer interactive states and more explicit copy.
  5. Retest the revised flows and include an accessibility review.

Testing limitations

The study involved seven participants and focused on four navigation and product-discovery tasks within a mobile prototype. Some misclicks were caused by prototype limitations, particularly the non-functional search interaction. The findings should therefore be considered directional and validated through further testing.

Reflection

Task completion alone does not indicate a frictionless experience. Observing user behaviour helped me move beyond positive ratings, challenge my assumptions, and prioritise the improvements that matter most.
