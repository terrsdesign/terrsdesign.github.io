01. Overview

Chocolate for Family is an ongoing UX/UI redesign project for an e-commerce platform serving both consumers and professionals in the chocolate and cocoa industry. The brand supports responsible cocoa cultivation and places sustainability and respect for raw materials at the heart of its mission.

The project focuses on creating a clearer and more coherent digital experience for two very different audiences, while improving the way products, information and services are structured and communicated.

The process started with preliminary research, interviews and surveys to validate initial assumptions and better understand the needs of both B2C and B2B users. The case study will continue to evolve as the project moves through strategy, information architecture, interface design and testing.

02. The Problem

The current experience lacks clarity at a structural and communication level.

The website serves both consumers and professionals, but this dual audience is not immediately evident.

Users struggle to understand who the experience is designed for, how the different product lines relate to each other and where to find the content relevant to their needs.

At the same time, brand-specific naming and highly descriptive copy often require interpretation instead of supporting immediate understanding, adding friction to product discovery and navigation.

03. Proposed Solution

The proposed solution will be shaped by the validated research findings and added as the project moves into the design phase.

04. Research

Research is currently in progress, and further details will be added as the project evolves. This section outlines the rationale behind the key choices made throughout the research phase.

Quick Audit

I conducted a quick audit to form an initial expert assessment of the current website, build an overall understanding of the experience, and identify the first usability, content, and structural issues to investigate further.

Initial User Testing

To reduce the influence of designer assumptions and validate the initial audit through external perspectives, I conducted three moderated user-testing sessions with one participant from each core audience: an industry professional, a chocolate expert, and an occasional consumer. Participants navigated the current website while thinking aloud and responding to five guided questions, allowing me to observe the difficulties and friction they encountered while completing the tasks.

Heuristic Evaluation

Following the initial user testing, I conducted a heuristic and visual evaluation based on Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics. This provided a systematic framework for reviewing the experience and identifying usability and visual consistency issues across the website.

Current Information Architecture

I mapped the current website’s information architecture to understand how its content, sections, and navigation are presently organized. This baseline will make it possible to identify structural issues and propose an alternative architecture during the redesign if the research confirms difficulties with navigation and content discovery.

Competitive Benchmark

I benchmarked companies with comparable B2B and B2C audiences to understand how they distinguish communication and navigation paths for different target groups. The selected companies also engage with sustainability, responsible sourcing, or respect for cocoa and its supply chain. Comparing their content structures and product information helped reveal useful patterns for managing a dual audience within one coherent experience.
